How a calling session actually goes.

The point of the tool is to get you from "I should do some outreach today" to a phone ringing in under a minute, without a spreadsheet in the middle.

  1. Step 01

    Pick an area and a trade

    Give it a pin code and a category — salons in Bandra, dentists in Andheri, gyms in Juhu. It reads the Google Maps listings for that area the way a customer searching for them would.

  2. Step 02

    Filter to the ones with no site

    Every listing is checked for a website field. The ones with nothing there are your list. These owners are already getting found on Maps — they just have nowhere to send the people who tap through.

  3. Step 03

    Work down the pipeline

    Each lead carries its rating, review count, area and number. Mark it Interested, Follow-up, Closed or Wrong number as you go, with your notes and the timestamp attached to that lead rather than to a row in a sheet.

  4. Step 04

    Pitch on WhatsApp

    One tap opens a WhatsApp thread with your pitch pre-filled, without that number ever entering your phone's address book. Most shop owners answer WhatsApp when they will not answer an unknown call.

What it does not do.

Worth knowing before you ask for access, so you are not expecting the wrong tool.

  • It does not send anything on its own. No bulk messaging, no auto-dialling, no sequences. You open each thread yourself and type to a real person.
  • It does not find email addresses. Google Maps listings rarely carry one, and guessing them is how you end up in spam folders.
  • It does not promise a lead count. How many businesses in an area lack a website is a fact about that area, not something a tool can inflate.
  • It is not a CRM. There is no invoicing, no proposals, no email sync. It covers the gap between a map listing and a first conversation, and stops there.

Questions before you ask for access.

Where does the business data come from?

From the public Google Maps listing itself — the same name, area, rating, review count and phone number any customer sees when they search. Nothing private is collected, and nothing is bought from a data broker.

How many leads will one search return?

It depends entirely on the area and the trade, and I would rather not quote a number I cannot promise. A dense commercial pin code with a trade that skews older — tailors, hardware shops, small clinics — turns up more website-less listings than a trade that is already online.

Do I need my own WhatsApp number?

Yes. The pitch opens in your own WhatsApp, from your own number, so the reply comes back to you. Use a number you are willing to put in front of strangers — a second SIM or a WhatsApp Business number is sensible.

Why is access invite-only?

Two reasons, both practical. Reading Maps data has a real cost per search, so an open signup would be throttled for everyone within a week. And if twenty people work the same pin code, the owners there get twenty identical pitches and stop answering. Spreading users across areas keeps the approach worth making.

What does it cost?

Pricing is settled per account when access is granted, because the cost depends on how much Maps data you actually pull. Message me on WhatsApp and I will tell you the number for your usage rather than quote an average.
